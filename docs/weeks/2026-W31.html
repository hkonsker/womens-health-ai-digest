Clinical AI AI Decision Support 9

AI-based clinician decision support system for diagnosis of inherited retinal diseases: a multicenter, randomized trial.

Nat Med · Jia H et al. · Jul 24, 2026

In a randomized trial across multiple countries, an AI tool that reads retina images to predict likely genetic causes of inherited eye diseases nearly doubled doctors' accuracy in guessing the right gene category compared to doctors working alone, and also improved their treatment decisions. This is a rare case of a clinical AI tool being tested with a real randomized comparison and a hard clinical outcome rather than just retrospective accuracy, making it a strong model for how AI decision support should be evaluated in any specialty, including reproductive genetics.

Clinical AI Speech Biomarker Validation 8

A deep learning model for speech-based prediction of clinical scores in people with Huntington's disease: a longitudinal study with cross-sectional replication.

Lancet Digit Health · Le Moine Veillon C et al. · Jul 23, 2026

Researchers built and tested an AI model that estimates Huntington's disease severity scores directly from voice recordings, validating it across multiple longitudinal cohorts and an independent replication group, and found it tracked disease progression nearly as well as clinician ratings and brain scans. This is a strong example of a rigorously validated AI biomarker with real prospective and external validation, showing how speech analysis could offer a cheap, noninvasive way to monitor neurodegenerative disease, a template relevant to tracking any chronic condition remotely.

Clinical AI Psychosis Risk Screening 6

Evaluating large language models for assessment of psychosis risk.

NPJ Digit Med · Zhu T et al. · Jul 23, 2026

Researchers tested several AI language models on real interview transcripts to see if they could identify people at high risk for psychosis and rate their symptoms, finding that larger models matched human raters reasonably well and rarely made things up. The study used a sizable multi-site sample and checked for fairness across demographic groups, which is a more rigorous test than typical single-site AI studies, though it still falls short of a clinical trial showing real-world impact.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Screening Efficiency 6

Improving laboratory workforce efficiency using AI-assisted digital cytology within an HPV-based cervical screening programme: A model-based evaluation for the NHS Cervical Screening Programmes.

BMJ Open · Wilson A et al. · Jul 28, 2026

A decision-tree modeling study estimated that AI-assisted digital cytology could cut the time needed to review cervical cancer screening slides from about 13 minutes to 4 minutes per slide across the NHS system, roughly a 69% gain in staff productivity. It is a policy-relevant, large-scale workforce analysis rather than a clinical trial, but it speaks directly to real deployment feasibility in a national screening program.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Cytology AI 6

An explainable machine learning approach for cervical cancer screening: decoding morphological diagnostic drivers.

Acta Clin Belg · Chang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

An interpretable machine learning tool for reading cervical cancer screening slides was tested against senior pathologists on 5,000 archived samples from a Chinese healthcare network, detecting high-grade lesions with 92.5% sensitivity versus 84.2% for manual review, while cutting review time by 63%. The performance held up across subgroups like menopausal or infected tissue, and the model's reasoning was traced to specific cell features, though it remains a single-network retrospective study without independent external validation.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Preeclampsia Prediction 6

Reply to the letter to the editor: "Machine learning model for predicting preeclampsia-related adverse outcomes".

Pregnancy Hypertens · Hackelöer M et al. · Jul 22, 2026

This is a reply to critics of a previously published machine learning model that predicts preeclampsia-related complications, clarifying that the model performed consistently well across a German and a North American patient group and adding it offers more information than the existing sFlt-1/PlGF blood test ratio alone. The response strengthens confidence in a model that has already undergone real cross-cohort validation, though a formal head-to-head comparison against the standard test is still pending.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Cancer Imaging 5

Multi-modal Stacking Machine Learning Model for Predicting Lymph Node Metastasis in Cervical Cancer.

Acad Radiol · Yang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

Researchers built a multi-step machine learning model that combines MRI image patterns and clinical data to predict whether cervical cancer has spread to lymph nodes, testing it on separate patient groups including an external one, with best accuracy scores around 0.87 to 0.92 on a 0 to 1 scale. It shows solid validation practice for a retrospective imaging study, but it stays in the incremental radiomics genre common to this type of work and offers no reproductive angle.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Oocyte Quality Biomarkers 5

AI-derived oocyte morphology and follicular fluid biomarkers in donors.

Reproduction · Herrero Y et al. · Jul 29, 2026

In 49 young egg donors, researchers found that AI-generated scores of egg quality correlated with certain metabolic and structural molecules measured in the fluid surrounding the egg, such as glucose, cholesterol, and a hormone called AMH. The study is small and exploratory, but it is a genuine attempt to ground an AI scoring tool in underlying biology rather than just image pattern matching, which is relevant to embryo and oocyte selection research.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI AI Chatbot Triage 5

Performance of Large Language Model-Based Chatbots in Primary Health Care Teleconsultations: A Comparison Between Human and Artificial Intelligence-Generated Responses.

Telemed J E Health · Barros GDM et al. · Jul 25, 2026

This study compared AI chatbot responses to real doctors' responses for over 900 patient telehealth questions in Brazil, finding AI responses were rated as easier to understand and performed comparably or better on most other quality measures across specialties including obstetrics and gynecology. It is a real head-to-head evaluation with a reasonably large sample, though it measures response quality rather than actual clinical outcomes.
