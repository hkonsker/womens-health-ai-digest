Clinical AI AI Genetic Diagnosis 9

AI-Based Clinician Decision Support System for Diagnosis of Inherited Retinal Diseases: A Multicenter, Randomized Trial.

Nat Med · Jia H et al. · Jul 24, 2026

In a multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT) of 295 patients with suspected inherited retinal disease (IRD), a Vision Transformer-based clinician decision support system (CDSS) called Retina4IRD, trained and externally validated across China, South Korea, and Poland on 1,843 patients, significantly improved top-5 genotype prediction accuracy when used alongside specialists versus specialists alone (88.5% vs 67.3%, P<0.001), and also improved downstream management scores. This is a rare example of an AI diagnostic tool demonstrating benefit in a prospective RCT with a hard clinical endpoint (management decisions) rather than just retrospective accuracy metrics, making it a strong template for how AI-assisted phenotyping-to-genotype pipelines could be validated in other specialties.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI tool that looks at eye scans and predicts which of 17 possible gene mutations is causing a rare inherited eye disease, and tested it by randomly assigning nearly 300 patients to either get help from this AI or not. When doctors used the AI's suggestions, they correctly narrowed down the genetic cause 88.5% of the time versus 67.3% without it, and patients also received better follow-up care decisions as a result. This is a strong study because it used a randomized trial design and tested the AI on patients from three different countries, showing the tool actually helps in real clinical decision-making, not just in lab tests.

Clinical AI Speech Biomarkers 8

A Deep Learning Model for Speech-Based Prediction of Clinical Scores in People with Huntington's Disease: A Longitudinal Study with Cross-Sectional Replication.

Lancet Digit Health · Le Moine Veillon C et al. · Jul 23, 2026

Researchers developed NDSNet, a deep learning model combining a pretrained wav2vec 2.0 speech encoder with a recurrent attentive network in a contrastive learning framework, to predict Unified Huntington's Disease Rating Scale (UHDRS) scores from speech across presymptomatic and symptomatic Huntington's disease stages, validated across three prospective cohorts plus an external replication cohort, achieving an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.87 versus 0.847 for clinician ratings. This is a rare example of a longitudinal, externally validated digital biomarker with individual-level sensitivity to disease progression, making it a strong model for how speech-based AI monitoring could be validated in other neurodegenerative and possibly reproductive-adjacent conditions requiring longitudinal tracking.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI system that listens to a person's speech and estimates how advanced their Huntington's disease is, tested across several groups of patients over many years including a separate outside group to check it still worked. The AI's estimates matched almost as well as scores given by human doctors, showing that speech alone can reliably track this brain disease over time without needing invasive tests.

Clinical AI AI Psychiatric Screening 6

Evaluating Large Language Models for Assessment of Psychosis Risk.

NPJ Digit Med · Zhu T et al. · Jul 23, 2026

Researchers evaluated 11 open-weight large language models (LLMs; deep learning models trained on large text corpora to process and generate language) on 678 clinical interview transcripts from 373 participants (77.7% at clinical high risk for psychosis, CHR-P), finding the best model (Llama-3.3-70B) achieved 80% accuracy, 93% sensitivity, and 58% specificity for CHR-P classification, with good agreement to researcher-rated symptom scores (intraclass correlation coefficient, ICC, of 0.74-0.75) and low rates of clinically relevant confabulation (3%). This is a multi-site benchmarking study rather than a prospective clinical trial, but it represents an early, reasonably rigorous test of LLMs for a scalable psychiatric screening task with real potential to extend specialist assessment capacity.

Explain this simply

Scientists tested 11 different AI language models on nearly 700 clinical interview transcripts to see if the AI could correctly identify people at high risk for developing psychosis, comparing the AI's judgments to those of trained human researchers. The best-performing AI got it right 80% of the time, agreed well with human experts on symptom severity, and rarely made up false information, suggesting AI could help expand access to this kind of specialized mental health screening, though this was a benchmarking exercise, not a test in real clinical use.

Multi-Modal Stacking Machine Learning Model for Predicting Lymph Node Metastasis in Cervical Cancer.

Acad Radiol · Yang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

A stacking ensemble model combining habitat radiomics from multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with clinical variables predicted lymph node metastasis in early-stage cervical cancer, reaching an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.875 in an external validation cohort of 125 patients, versus 0.845-0.882 for the best single base classifier. The multi-cohort design with external validation and decision curve analysis strengthens confidence, but the study remains retrospective and needs prospective surgical outcome testing before it can guide lymph node dissection decisions.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a multi-step AI model that combines MRI scan patterns with patient data to predict whether early-stage cervical cancer has spread to lymph nodes, and it correctly distinguished patients about 88 percent of the time even in a separate group of patients used to test it. This is a more rigorous kind of testing than most AI cancer studies get, but it still needs to be tried in real treatment decisions before it changes practice.

Improving Laboratory Workforce Efficiency Using AI-Assisted Digital Cytology Within an HPV-Based Cervical Screening Programme: A Model-Based Evaluation for the NHS Cervical Screening Programmes.

BMJ Open · Wilson A et al. · Jul 28, 2026

A decision-tree model of the National Health Service (NHS) cervical screening cytology workflow estimated that AI-assisted digital cytology would cut per-slide review time from 12.9 to 4.0 minutes, reducing annual staff hours by roughly two-thirds across 479,125 slides. This is a policy-relevant health-economic simulation rather than a clinical trial, so its conclusions depend on the accuracy of the input parameters, but it offers a concrete operational case for AI-assisted screening at national scale.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a computer simulation of how the UK's cervical cancer screening system processes lab samples, and estimated that adding an AI tool to review cell samples would cut the time each sample takes from about 13 minutes to 4 minutes, saving a huge amount of staff time nationwide. It is not a real-world trial, just a modeling estimate, but it makes a strong practical case for why health systems facing staff shortages might want to adopt this kind of AI.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Cervical Screening AI 6

An Explainable Machine Learning Approach for Cervical Cancer Screening: Decoding Morphological Diagnostic Drivers.

Acta Clin Belg · Chang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

An interpretable machine learning (ML) pipeline for liquid-based cytology (LBC) achieved 92.5% sensitivity for detecting high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions or worse (HSIL+), significantly higher than 84.2% for a double-blind panel of senior cytopathologists on a retrospective set of 5,000 women, with no performance drop across menopausal or infection subgroups and a 63% reduction in median review time. The head-to-head comparison against expert pathologists and subgroup robustness analysis are notable strengths, though the single-network retrospective design still needs prospective, multi-site confirmation before influencing screening workflows.

Explain this simply

An AI tool for reading cervical cancer screening slides correctly caught more cases of serious pre-cancerous changes than a panel of experienced pathologists did, 92.5 percent versus 84.2 percent, in a study of 5,000 women, and it worked just as well regardless of a woman's menopause status or infections, while also cutting review time by nearly two thirds. This is a fairly strong test because it directly compared the AI against expert humans, but it was done at only one healthcare network using past data, so it still needs to be confirmed in real-time use across different hospitals.

Predictive Modeling with Machine Learning for Low Birth Weight in the State of São Paulo: A Retrospective Population-Based Study.

Sao Paulo Med J · Almeida AVA et al. · Jul 24, 2026

A retrospective population-based study of 2.55 million live births in São Paulo state used logistic regression and random forest models to predict low birth weight from sociodemographic and prenatal care variables, with the balanced logistic regression model achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) of 0.850. The dataset's scale is a strength for generalizability within this population, but the study lacks external validation and offers no new algorithmic or clinical intervention, limiting its actionability beyond confirming known risk factors.

Explain this simply

Using records from over 2.5 million births in a Brazilian state, researchers built simple prediction models to flag babies at risk of being born underweight, based on things like the mother's age, education, and prenatal care, and their best model got it right in a reasonably reliable way about 85 percent of the time on a measure combining true and false positives. The huge sample size is impressive, but the study mostly confirms known risk factors like teen pregnancy and limited prenatal care rather than revealing anything new, and the model was not tested on a separate population.

Performance of Large Language Model-Based Chatbots in Primary Health Care Teleconsultations: A Comparison Between Human and Artificial Intelligence-Generated Responses.

Telemed J E Health · Barros GDM et al. · Jul 25, 2026

A blinded, cross-sectional comparison of 180 real human versus large language model (LLM)-generated responses to primary care teleconsultation queries across cardiology, endocrinology, and obstetrics/gynecology found artificial intelligence (AI) scored significantly higher on comprehensibility with no significant differences in medical adequacy or risk potential. This is a real head-to-head evaluation with blinded raters rather than a benchmark exercise, though the sample size per specialty and lack of patient outcome data limit how far it generalizes to real-world safety.

Explain this simply

Researchers compared answers given by real doctors versus an AI chatbot to patient questions sent through a telehealth program, and found the AI's answers were easier to understand while being just as medically sound and safe. This is a fairly solid comparison because it used blinded judges, but it still only measured written answer quality, not real patient outcomes.
