LLMs and Patient Communication Portal Summaries RCT 8

Embedding LLMs in the Patient Portal to Summarize Acute Minor Illness Information: A Three-Arm Experimental Study.

Int J Med Inform · Esmaeilzadeh P · Oct 1, 2026

This three-arm experimental study embedded two frontier large language models (LLMs) into a primary care patient portal to generate plain-language summaries of acute illness information, finding significantly higher comprehension scores, portal engagement, and self-management adherence versus standard portal access, plus a 61% lower odds of unnecessary return visits (odds ratio 0.34). This is a rare randomized comparison with a hard utilization endpoint and clinician-audited safety data, making it one of the more substantive patient-facing LLM evaluations this cycle.

Explain this simply

Researchers randomly assigned patients to get AI-written summaries of their visit information (from two different chatbot models) or the usual portal text, and found the AI summaries led to much better understanding of their illness, more use of the patient portal, and 61% fewer unnecessary follow-up visits. This is notable because it is a real controlled experiment with an outcome that matters, not just a rating of how good the chatbot's answers sounded.

Large Language Models (LLMs)

AI systems trained on huge amounts of text that can understand and generate human-like language.

Odds Ratio

A number comparing the odds of an event happening in one group versus another, where 1 means no difference and lower than 1 means the event is less likely in the first group.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Fetal Monitoring AI 7

Deep Time-To-Event Models for Intrapartum Fetal Monitoring.

medRxiv · Imai Aldeia GS et al. · Sep 26, 2026

This study reframes intrapartum fetal heart rate monitoring (cardiotocography, CTG) as a time-to-event prediction problem rather than assuming a fixed pre-delivery window, developing a deep learning model called Marked DeepHit that jointly predicts imminent delivery and fetal acidosis, achieving test-set area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.837 to 0.935 at 6 and 12 hours into labor, with validation at a second external site. The external validation and the novel time-to-event framing that avoids the unrealistic assumption of known delivery timing make this a methodologically meaningful advance toward real-world deployable intrapartum monitoring, though it remains a proof-of-principle rather than a deployed clinical tool.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI model that continuously watches a baby's heart rate during labor and predicts, at any point in time, how likely the baby is to be delivered soon and whether it might be in distress, without needing to know in advance when delivery will happen. The model was tested at a second hospital different from where it was built, which is an important sign that it might work well in real-world settings beyond where it was trained.

Time-To-Event Prediction

A statistical approach that estimates when something will happen, like a delivery, rather than just whether it will happen.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Fetal Monitoring AI 7

CTG-FRAME: Development and Validation of a Multi‑modal Deep Learning for Intrapartum Escalation Support and Severe Fetal Acidemia Prediction.

BMC Med Inform Decis Mak · Tjondronegoro D et al. · Sep 28, 2026

CTG-FRAME is a multi-module deep learning framework for predicting severe fetal acidemia from cardiotocography (CTG) signals, trained and cross-validated on the CTU-UHB dataset (n=552) and externally tested on a separate multicenter cohort (SPaM, n=300), achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.888 with sensitivity 0.85 and specificity 0.964, and showing preserved calibration without retuning on external data. This is a genuine external validation of an intrapartum decision-support tool addressing a well-known clinical problem, high false-positive burden in fetal monitoring, making it one of the stronger obstetric AI entries this week, though the acidemia prevalence differs notably between cohorts.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI system that reads fetal heart rate and contraction patterns during labor to flag babies at risk of dangerously low blood oxygen, and tested it on a different hospital's patients than the ones it was trained on, correctly identifying 85 percent of true cases. This matters because current fetal monitoring causes a lot of false alarms, and testing on an outside hospital's data is a meaningful check that the tool might generalize, though the two patient groups had different rates of the condition.

Clinical AI Algorithmic Fairness 6

Evaluating the Impact of Social Determinants of Health on Model Performance and Fairness in Artificial Intelligence for Glaucoma Progression Prediction.

J Am Med Inform Assoc · Lin SYT et al. · Sep 30, 2026

This multicenter study of 63,268 glaucoma patients across 12 centers tested whether adding community-level social determinants of health (SDoH, such as area deprivation and rural-urban location) to an XGBoost model predicting progression to glaucoma surgery improved algorithmic fairness, finding reduced generalized equalized odds (GEO, a fairness metric comparing error rates across subgroups) at the internal site and one external site but not at two demographically different external sites. The large multicenter design with external validation is a methodological strength, and the finding that fairness gains do not transfer across sites is directly relevant to anyone deploying prediction models across health systems.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested whether adding neighborhood-level information, like local poverty levels and whether an area is rural or urban, to an AI model predicting who needs glaucoma surgery would make the model treat different patient groups more fairly. Across more than 63,000 patients at 12 medical centers, the extra information helped fairness at some locations but not others, showing that a fix that works in one hospital system may not work in another.

Clinical AI Deterioration Prediction 6

MADE: Leveraging Informative Missingness and Temporal Evaluation for Electronic Health Record-Based Deterioration Prediction in General Wards.

J Am Med Inform Assoc · Mun Y et al. · Sep 30, 2026

This study developed Masked-Attention-based timestamp-wise Data Embedding (MADE), a missingness-aware transformer model for predicting deterioration in general hospital wards, validated across one internal and two external cohorts, achieving area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.966/0.902/0.825 for major adverse events and 0.934/0.874/0.776 for sepsis, with the no-imputation configuration outperforming standard imputation methods. The multi-site external validation and the finding that missingness itself carries predictive signal are methodologically notable, though calibration degraded at higher risk levels, suggesting site-specific recalibration is needed before deployment.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI model that predicts when hospital patients on general wards are about to get seriously worse, using the pattern of which measurements are missing as a clue rather than just filling in the gaps. Tested at three different hospitals, the model worked quite well, especially for catching major health crises, but its confidence in high-risk predictions wasn't perfectly matched to reality, meaning it would need fine-tuning before being trusted in each new hospital.

The Building and Application of a Machine Learning Model on Predicting Euploid Blastocysts in IVF Treatments Without PGT-A.

Reprod Sci · Yuan Z et al. · Oct 2, 2026

This study built a logistic regression-based machine learning model using preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A) results, morphokinetic development, morphology, and maternal age to predict blastocyst euploidy, achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.877 on an independent validation set and a lower false positive rate than Gardner grading or KIDScore D5; frozen embryo transfer outcomes improved when embryos were selected by the model versus conventional morphology grading. The comparison against established clinical grading systems and the reported improvement in a real treatment outcome make this more actionable than typical embryo-selection models, though details on study design and cohort size are sparse.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI tool that predicts whether an embryo has the normal number of chromosomes without needing an invasive genetic test, using things like how the embryo develops, its shape, and the mother's age. When doctors used this tool to pick embryos for transfer, pregnancy outcomes were better than when they used standard visual grading alone, which is a meaningful real-world result.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Endometrial Histology AI 6

Foundation Model-Powered Deep Learning of Endometrial Histology for Predicting the Cumulative Live Birth of an in Vitro Fertilization Cycle.

PLOS Digit Health · Xu N et al. · Sep 28, 2026

This study compared an end-to-end convolutional network against a frozen foundation model feature extractor (UNI2-h) for predicting cumulative live birth from endometrial histology images in in vitro fertilization (IVF) patients, finding the foundation model approach generalized better to an external phase-based test cohort (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, AUROC, of 0.90 versus near-random for the standard model) despite lower performance on the internal outcome-based task. The divergence between internal and external performance highlights a genuine generalization problem relevant to anyone building histology-based prediction tools, and the finding that pretrained foundation models transfer better is a useful methodological signal even though absolute performance (AUROC 0.74 internally) remains modest for clinical use.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested two types of AI that look at microscope images of the womb lining to predict whether an IVF cycle will lead to a live birth, comparing a standard image-reading AI to one built on a large pretrained AI model. The pretrained model held up much better when tested on new data from a different context, while the standard model performed barely better than guessing, showing that some AI designs generalize better than others even if neither is yet accurate enough for routine clinical use.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Radiotherapy Automation 6

A Hybrid Deep Learning Framework for Automated Treatment Planning in Cervical Cancer Radiotherapy.

J Appl Clin Med Phys · Jiang H et al. · Oct 1, 2026

This study developed a hybrid automated deep learning-based plan optimization (HALO) framework for cervical cancer intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT), combining a fluence prediction network with Monte Carlo dose calculation, tested on 20 held-out patients with patient-specific quality assurance (QA) showing a 97.4% gamma passing rate, reduced bladder and small intestine dose, and planning time under 3 minutes versus the typical trial-and-error process. This is a concrete demonstration of clinically feasible, substantially faster automated radiotherapy planning with real dosimetric and QA verification, which is a meaningful advance in clinical AI for oncology even outside reproductive medicine specifically.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI system that can automatically design radiation treatment plans for cervical cancer in under 3 minutes, instead of the usual lengthy trial-and-error process done by human planners. The AI plans reduced radiation exposure to nearby organs like the bladder and intestine while passing standard safety checks, suggesting this could make high-quality cancer treatment planning faster and more consistent.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Embryo Selection AI 6

Head-To-Head Comparison of Six Commercial Artificial Intelligence Algorithms for Embryo Ploidy Risk Stratification in 1000 PGT-A-Tested Blastocysts.

Reprod Biomed Online · Giménez-Rodríguez C et al. · Oct 1, 2026

This retrospective single-center cohort directly compared six commercial artificial intelligence (AI) embryo scoring algorithms against preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A) ground truth in 1000 blastocysts, finding all six converged to similar moderate discrimination (AUC 0.713-0.744) but showed only moderate rank concordance with each other, meaning different algorithms would prioritize different embryos for transfer. This is a useful head-to-head benchmark showing that commercial embryo AI tools are not interchangeable and should be treated as relative risk stratifiers rather than diagnostic substitutes for PGT-A, a finding directly actionable for clinics choosing or combining these systems.

Explain this simply

Scientists tested six different commercial AI tools that score embryos for chromosomal normalcy against the gold-standard genetic test, using 1000 embryos from a single fertility clinic. All six tools performed similarly overall but often disagreed on which specific embryos looked best, showing that choosing one AI tool over another can change which embryo gets transferred, and that none of these tools should replace genetic testing when it's available.

The system performed better than standard methods and even improved on a hospital it had never seen before, which matters for standardizing radiation planning in gynecologic cancer care, though it only covers contouring, not outcomes like survival.

LLMs and Patient Communication Deployed Patient Chatbot 6

OsteoCHAT: Real-World Patient Evaluation and Benchmarking of a Guideline-Grounded Osteoporosis Chatbot.

Osteoporos Int · Schulz N et al. · Oct 3, 2026

This multicenter real-world evaluation deployed OsteoCHAT, a retrieval-augmented, guideline-grounded large language model (LLM) chatbot, to 1,075 real patient-chatbot interactions across seven German centers, finding 97.4% positive feedback and expert-rated answer quality comparable to general-purpose LLMs, with identified weaknesses in pharmacological and safety-related topics. Real deployment with patient feedback and expert benchmarking against general LLMs, rather than another rubric-only simulation, makes this a more substantive contribution to this beat, though trust scores were comparatively lower and no control group or outcome measure beyond satisfaction was used.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a chatbot grounded in official osteoporosis treatment guidelines and had real patients use it across seven medical centers in Germany, collecting over a thousand actual question-and-answer interactions. Patients mostly liked it and found it as good as general AI chatbots, but experts found it struggled with explaining medication risks and rare safety issues, which matters because this was tested on real patients rather than simulated cases.

J Multidiscip Healthc · Ransinyo K et al. · Sep 26, 2026

This quasi-experimental pretest-posttest study tested a 12-week chatbot-based hypertension management program against usual care in 68 community-dwelling older adults, finding significant improvements in knowledge, medication adherence, and blood pressure control (adjusted systolic 127.4 vs 135.8 mmHg) in the intervention group. The measured effect on a hard clinical endpoint, blood pressure, is a meaningful step beyond typical rubric-based chatbot studies, though the non-randomized design with recruitment from different healthcare settings limits causal certainty.

Explain this simply

This study tested a chatbot program that helped older adults manage high blood pressure through education and reminders over 12 weeks, comparing it to a group that just got a standard pamphlet. The chatbot group showed real improvements in blood pressure readings and medication habits, which is a genuine health outcome rather than just a quality score, though the study was not a randomized trial so the comparison could be influenced by other differences between the groups.

LLMs and Patient Communication AI Versus Clinicians 6

Artificial Intelligence and Vascular Surgeons in Patient Communication: A Comparative Analysis of Intelligibility and Clinical Appropriateness in Acute Deep Vein Thrombosis.

Phlebology · Gómez Gutiérrez OA et al. · Oct 1, 2026

This observational cross-sectional study directly compared patient-facing explanations of acute deep vein thrombosis (DVT) from 11 board-certified vascular surgeons against 11 distinct large language models (LLMs), blinded and scored on clinical appropriateness and readability, finding no significant difference in appropriateness (median 7/8 both) but substantially lower variability and higher readability in LLM responses. This is a genuine clinician comparator study, the kind that is more discriminating and useful for this beat, though the sample of surgeon responses is small and the setting is Spanish-language and single-vignette.

Explain this simply

This study had real vascular surgeons and various AI chatbots each write an explanation of a blood clot diagnosis for patients, then had graders blindly compare them. The AI responses were judged just as medically appropriate as the surgeons' explanations, and were more consistently clear and readable, though both groups missed mentioning a key related risk, which is a notable finding because it directly pits AI against real doctors rather than just scoring AI answers alone.
