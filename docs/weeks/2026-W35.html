Clinical AI AI ECG Screening 8

Electrocardiogram-Based Deep Learning to Prioritize Testing for Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy.

JAMA · Croon PM et al. · Aug 28, 2026

This diagnostic test study developed and validated a deep learning model reading electrocardiogram (ECG) images to flag transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.84 internally and 0.78-0.89 across five external multinational cohorts, plus three prospective screening cohorts, with sequential AI-ECG plus AI-echocardiography raising positive predictive value from 0.24 to 0.66. The scale of external and prospective screening validation, and the local deployability from routine ECG images rather than raw signals, make this a strong example of AI moving toward real-world case-finding for an underdiagnosed, treatable disease.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI tool that reads regular heart tracings (ECGs) to spot a serious but treatable heart disease called transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, which is often missed. The tool worked well across multiple countries and in real screening groups of older patients, and combining it with an AI heart ultrasound check made the positive results much more trustworthy, which matters because it could help doctors find hidden cases earlier.

Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM)

A heart disease caused by an abnormal protein building up in heart tissue, which is treatable if caught early but often missed.

Electrocardiogram (ECG)

A recording of the heart's electrical activity, commonly used to check heart rhythm and structure.

Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC)

A score from 0.5 to 1 showing how well a test distinguishes people with a condition from those without it, where higher is better.

Positive Predictive Value

The chance that a positive test result is actually correct, which depends on how common the condition is in the tested group.

Clinical AI AI Ultrasound Screening 8

Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Acquisition and Interpretation for Screening Aortic Stenosis.

JAMA Cardiol · Lee E et al. · Aug 28, 2026

This diagnostic study developed a deep learning algorithm for detecting moderate or greater aortic stenosis (AS) from AI-guided focused cardiac ultrasound (FoCUS), then prospectively tested it with novice operators across multiple Mayo Clinic sites, achieving 93% sensitivity and 96% specificity with 96.6% of scans usable, and expert review raising positive predictive value from 49.4% to 91.1%. The prospective, multi-site validation with non-expert operators demonstrates a real path toward decentralized valve disease screening, a genuine capability advance beyond retrospective algorithm development.

Explain this simply

Researchers created an AI system that guides untrained staff to take heart ultrasound images and then automatically checks for a common valve problem called aortic stenosis. When tested in real time with inexperienced operators at several hospital locations, the system correctly identified the disease most of the time, showing that AI could let heart screening happen without needing a highly trained ultrasound expert on site.

Aortic Stenosis (AS)

A narrowing of the heart's aortic valve that restricts blood flow and can become life threatening if untreated.

Focused Cardiac Ultrasound (FoCUS)

A quick, simplified heart ultrasound exam meant to be done by non-expert operators rather than trained sonographers.

Sensitivity

Specificity

Positive Predictive Value

Clinical AI EEG Foundation Model 8

Toward Unified and Comprehensive Automated Electroencephalogram Interpretation: A Multicentre Development and Validation of an Electroencephalogram Foundation Model.

Lancet Digit Health · Sun C et al. · Aug 25, 2026

This multicentre study developed a foundation model for electroencephalogram (EEG) interpretation trained on 18,677 patients and externally validated on 1573 patients across 48 institutions, achieving area under the curve (AUC) scores of 0.86-0.98 across 17 findings and matching or exceeding expert consensus on inter-rater reliability (IRR) analysis. The breadth of tasks covered, the scale of external validation across nearly 50 institutions, and the direct comparison to human expert agreement make this a substantial step toward deployable general-purpose EEG interpretation rather than another narrow single-task model.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a broad AI system that can read brain wave recordings (EEGs) and flag many different types of neurological problems, not just one narrow task like most previous AI tools. It was tested at nearly 50 hospitals around the world and performed about as well as trained human experts, and sometimes better, which suggests it could genuinely help extend expert-level brain reading to places that lack specialists.

Clinical AI ATTR-CM Prognosis 7

Machine Learning-Driven Risk Prediction Model in Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy.

JAMA Cardiol · Baj G et al. · Aug 28, 2026

This multicenter cohort study built a random survival forest machine learning (ML) model predicting mortality and heart failure hospitalization in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), validated via internal-external cross-validation across three European cohorts (n=850), with Harrell concordance indices of 0.72-0.77, outperforming existing Mayo and National Amyloidosis Centre staging systems by 2-19% depending on metric. The rigorous held-out validation across centers strengthens confidence in generalizability, though the improvement over existing staging systems, while real, is incremental rather than transformative.

Explain this simply

Scientists created a computer model that predicts how long patients with a specific serious heart disease will survive and whether they will need hospital care, tested across different hospitals in Switzerland and Austria. The new model did modestly better than the standard scoring systems doctors already use, which is a real but not dramatic improvement in predicting patient outcomes.

Machine Learning (ML)

A branch of AI in which computer models learn patterns from data rather than being explicitly programmed with rules.

Random Survival Forest

A machine learning method that predicts how long until an event, like death, happens by combining many decision trees built on time-to-event data.

Internal-External Cross-Validation

A testing method where a model is repeatedly trained on some hospital sites and tested on a site left out, to check if it generalizes beyond its original data.

Harrell Concordance Index

A statistic measuring how well a model correctly ranks which patients will have an event sooner versus later, where 1.0 is perfect and 0.5 is no better than chance.

Multimodal Artificial Intelligence-Based Long-Term Mortality Prediction After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation: A Multicentre Development, Validation, and Testing Study.

Lancet Digit Health · Shiri I et al. · Aug 27, 2026

This multicentre study developed multimodal artificial intelligence (AI) models integrating clinical, imaging, and procedural data to predict long-term mortality after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in 3991 patients from Switzerland and Japan, with external testing at the second center and comparison against established surgical risk scores like the Society of Thoracic Surgeons Predicted Risk of Mortality (STS-PROM). The multimodal data integration and true geographic external validation against clinical standard-of-care risk scores represents a methodologically solid advance, though as with most survival prediction models the incremental benefit over existing scores needs confirmation in more diverse populations.

Explain this simply

They tested the model on patients from a different country than where it was built and found it did better than the standard risk calculators doctors currently use, which could improve how doctors plan long-term care after this procedure.

Non-Inferiority Analysis of a Fetal Heart Rate Artificial Intelligence Algorithm to Registered Nurse Assessment.

Pregnancy (Hoboken) · Pardasani R et al. · Aug 27, 2026

This retrospective non-inferiority study compared an artificial intelligence (AI) algorithm's labeling of cardiotocography (CTG) fetal heart rate and uterine activity data against registered nurse (RN) readers across 800 tracings from five US hospitals, finding the algorithm met the 15% non-inferiority threshold for most endpoints except contraction duration. This is a genuine head-to-head comparison against real clinical staff on a common obstetric monitoring task, though the retrospective design and single comparator group (RNs rather than physicians) limit how strongly it supports full clinical deployment.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested whether an AI system that reads fetal heart rate monitors during labor performs about as well as trained nurses at spotting important patterns, using 800 real tracings from five hospitals. The AI mostly matched nurse performance except for measuring how long contractions lasted, suggesting AI could help support this type of monitoring, though the study looked backward at existing data rather than testing it in real time.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Obstetric Risk Prediction 7

Machine Learning-Based First-Trimester Antenatal Risk Prediction for Adverse Maternal and Neonatal Outcomes: Multicenter Model Development Study.

J Med Internet Res · Li S et al. · Aug 26, 2026

This multicenter model development study trained separate machine learning models (LightGBM, light gradient boosting machine) on first-trimester data from over 700,000 pregnancies across Sweden, Chile, and Singapore, showing improved discrimination (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, AUROC, of 0.60 to 0.65) over existing guideline- or clinician-based risk assessment (AUROC 0.52 to 0.56) for a composite adverse maternal and neonatal outcome. The large multinational scale and direct comparison against real-world clinical practice make this one of the stronger obstetric risk prediction studies this cycle, though the absolute discrimination remains modest and only internally validated within each country's own data.

Explain this simply

Researchers built AI models using first-trimester pregnancy data from over 700,000 women in three countries to predict which pregnancies would have serious complications, and the models did modestly better than the risk checklists doctors and midwives currently use. This is a large, real-world comparison showing AI can beat current practice at least a little, though the improvement is not dramatic and the models were only tested within the same countries where they were built.

This diagnostic study trained a convolutional neural network on 4978 wound images to detect surgical site infections (SSI) and externally validated it on 407 prospectively collected images, achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.91 internally and 0.82 externally with a positive decision curve analysis. LLMs and Patient Communication AI Versus Therapist 6

Large Language Models as a New Tool for Therapists in Internet-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy: Blinded Clinician Rating Pilot Experiment.

JMIR Ment Health · Lamm TT et al. · Aug 26, 2026

In a preregistered blinded clinician rating experiment, 54 raters compared human versus large language model (LLM)-generated therapist responses within a simulated internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy (iCBT) protocol, finding statistically similar quality across dimensions (overall quality d=0.06, empathy d=0.42) and that raters correctly identified human responses more often (79%) than LLM ones (63%). This is a genuine blinded comparison against real clinician output with a preregistered design, which is the kind of evidence this beat should prioritize, though the patient messages were fictitious rather than real clinical cases.

Explain this simply

Trained raters, without knowing which was which, compared therapy responses written by real therapists versus by an AI to the same simulated patient messages, and found the AI's answers were rated about as good as the humans', sometimes even more empathetic, though people could still guess right which was human most of the time. This matters because it is one of the few studies that actually pits AI against real clinicians in a blinded, planned way rather than just grading AI on a checklist.

Large Language Model (LLM)

A type of AI trained on large amounts of text that can generate humanlike written responses.

Internet-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (iCBT)

A structured form of talk therapy delivered online, often through written exchanges with a therapist.

Preregistered

A study plan that was written down and publicly filed before the research was conducted, to prevent changing methods after seeing results.

Blinded

A study design feature where evaluators do not know which responses came from which source, reducing bias in judging quality.

Physician-Revised AI-Generated Drafts Are Associated with Higher Ratings of Written Explanations in End-Of-Life Care in the Intensive Care Unit: A Scenario-Based Single-Center Cross-Sectional Study.

J Intensive Care · Kokita A et al. · Aug 24, 2026

In a scenario-based cross-sectional study with 45 raters, physician-revised drafts of artificial intelligence (AI)-generated responses to simulated intensive care unit (ICU) end-of-life questions received significantly higher ratings for medical validity and empathy than either physician-only or AI-only responses, with the empathy gain being more pronounced. This directly tests a collaborative human-AI workflow against both pure-human and pure-AI output on a high-stakes communication task, which is more informative than typical rubric-only studies, though it is single-center and scenario-based rather than drawn from real patient encounters.

Explain this simply

In a study using pretend end-of-life ICU scenarios, when doctors edited AI-written explanations before sending them, those combined answers were rated as more caring and medically sound than answers written by either doctors alone or AI alone. This suggests that having a doctor revise AI drafts could improve difficult conversations, but the scenarios were simulated, not real patient cases, so the real-world impact is still unclear.

Predictive Models of Pregnancy Based on Male Partner Data From a Preconception Cohort Study.

Andrology · Talaei M et al. · Aug 27, 2026

This prospective preconception cohort study used machine learning (ML) on 185 male partner variables from 2843 couples to predict spontaneous conception, with the best logistic regression models achieving area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) around 0.64-0.65, modestly better than chance but far from strong discrimination. The large, well-characterized prospective cohort and novel focus on male-partner predictors are notable strengths, though the modest predictive performance limits immediate clinical applicability.

Explain this simply

Researchers studied nearly 2900 couples trying to conceive and used AI to see if information about the male partner's lifestyle and health could predict how quickly pregnancy would happen. The models were only modestly accurate, correctly ranking outcomes a bit better than a coin flip, so while this highlights useful factors like prior pregnancies and smoking exposure, it is not yet a reliable prediction tool.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Embryo Selection AI 5

Expert Fusion Network for Automated Blastocyst Morphology and IVF Decision Support.

IEEE J Biomed Health Inform · Zia A et al. · Aug 26, 2026

This study developed the Expert Fusion Network (EFN), a mixture-of-experts deep learning architecture with a U-Net segmentation backbone, to classify and segment trophectoderm, blastocoel, and inner cell mass in blastocyst images, achieving F1-scores of 0.90 to 0.97 and Dice similarity coefficients around 0.90 to 0.92 with cross-dataset generalization on an independent set of 2,344 annotated images. The cross-dataset testing is a meaningful step beyond typical single-center embryo grading models, but the authors themselves note prospective clinical validation against IVF outcomes is still needed before this can inform embryo selection decisions.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI system that automatically identifies and measures the key parts of an early embryo (a small fluid pocket, an outer cell layer, and a cluster of cells that becomes the fetus) from microscope images, and it matched expert-drawn outlines quite closely, even when tested on images from a different clinic. This is a promising step toward more consistent embryo grading for IVF, but the researchers have not yet shown it improves actual pregnancy or birth outcomes.

Automating the Management of Extraspinal Findings in Magnetic Resonance Imaging Spine Studies Using a Privacy-Preserving Large Language Model: Retrospective Validation Study.

J Med Internet Res · Ong W et al. · Aug 26, 2026

In this retrospective validation study, a privacy-preserving large language model (PP-LLM) automatically detected extraspinal findings on 400 spine magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reports, correctly identifying 99.8% of findings and all clinically urgent cases, with 96.3% accuracy on specialty referral decisions and near-perfect agreement (Gwet's kappa 0.959) with clinician review. This is a solid general clinical AI workflow application with strong numbers on a modest single-center retrospective sample, but it has no reproductive focus beyond gynecologic findings being one incidental category and needs prospective, multi-site testing before workflow deployment.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested an AI language model that reads radiology reports and patient records to catch findings outside the spine, like unexpected kidney or gynecologic problems, that doctors might need to follow up on. It caught nearly every important finding and matched human reviewers closely, which could reduce missed follow-ups, but the study only looked backward at one hospital's past reports rather than testing the tool going forward in real time.

NICE: A Two-Step Non-Invasive Framework for Embryo cfDNA Read Enrichment and Quality Assessment.

Adv Sci (Weinh) · Zhou X et al. · Aug 24, 2026

This study introduced NICE, a two-step framework combining an improved cell-free DNA (cfDNA) purification algorithm (DECENT-plus) with machine learning classifiers to reduce maternal DNA contamination in non-invasive preimplantation genetic testing (niPGT) from spent embryo culture medium, aiming to improve embryo ploidy assessment and quality classification. The technical contribution addresses a known contamination problem that limits clinical adoption of niPGT, but the abstract reports no external clinical outcome data such as implantation or live birth rates tied to the framework's classifications.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a two-step method that cleans up genetic material shed by embryos into their culture fluid, removing contamination from the mother's own DNA, and then uses machine learning to judge embryo quality without needing an invasive biopsy. This could make embryo screening safer and easier for IVF patients, but the study does not yet show whether using this method actually leads to more successful pregnancies.

LLMs and Patient Communication LLM Hallucination Risk 5

Evaluating Large Language Models in Endodontic Irrigants: A Structured Assessment of Accuracy, Readability, Hallucination Subtypes and Clinical Risk.

J Endod · Erkal D et al. · Aug 28, 2026

This study systematically evaluated four large language models (LLMs), ChatGPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Pro, and DeepSeek 3.2, on 100 endodontic irrigation questions each, scoring accuracy, hallucination subtype, clinical risk, and ten-day test-retest stability, finding DeepSeek had the highest hallucination rate (29%) and 16% critical-risk outputs while hallucination strongly correlated with clinical risk (rho 0.93). The hallucination-subtyping and temporal stability analysis is a more rigorous failure-mode characterization than typical single-snapshot rubric scoring, though it still lacks a comparison against real clinician answers to patients.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested four different AI chatbots on 100 questions about a dental procedure step, checking not just whether answers were accurate but also how often they made up false information and how risky those errors could be for patients, then retested ten days later to see if answers stayed consistent. One chatbot made things up nearly a third of the time with some dangerous mistakes, showing that not all AI models are equally safe to trust for clinical information, though the study still did not compare the chatbots to what a real dentist would tell a patient.

Large Language Model (LLM)

A type of AI trained on large amounts of text that can generate humanlike written responses.

Hallucination

When an AI model generates information that sounds plausible but is factually incorrect or made up.

Test-Retest Stability
