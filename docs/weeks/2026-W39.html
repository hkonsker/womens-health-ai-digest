Clinical AI Diagnostic Decision Support 7

Artificial Intelligence-Based Diagnostic Decision Support for Primary Care of Older Adults Using Collective Intelligence and Imitation Learning.

J Am Med Inform Assoc · Streiffer CD et al. · Sep 24, 2026

Investigators trained a multi-label neural network on 707,598 primary care encounters of adults 65 and older using imitation learning to mimic clinician diagnostic and ordering decisions, then validated it with a randomized, blinded equivalence study showing diagnostic recommendations were statistically equivalent to actual clinician practice, though order recommendations were not. This is one of the few diagnostic decision support system (DDSS) studies to move beyond retrospective discrimination metrics to a prospective clinician-comparison design, which is exactly the kind of evidence this reader should track even though order-level equivalence failed.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI tool that learns to suggest diagnoses and medical orders by copying what real doctors do, trained on over 700,000 visits by older patients. In a careful test comparing the AI's suggestions to what doctors actually did, the diagnosis suggestions matched real practice well, but the suggestions for tests and orders did not match as closely, showing the tool still needs work before it could support decisions in the clinic.

Imitation Learning

A machine learning approach where a model learns to reproduce the decisions of human experts rather than being told the single correct answer.

Equivalence Study

A study designed to show that two things perform similarly to each other, rather than trying to prove one is better than the other.

Diagnostic Decision Support System (DDSS)

Software intended to help clinicians choose diagnoses or next steps by analyzing patient data.

AI in OB/GYN and REI AI Radiotherapy Delineation 7

AI-Based Delineation Based on Clinical Data in MR-Guided Brachytherapy for Cervical Cancer Reduces Delineation Time - A Prospective Paired Trial.

Phys Med · Konrad ML et al. · Sep 22, 2026

A prospective paired trial in 32 patients undergoing MR-guided cervical cancer brachytherapy used an nnU-Net deep learning model trained on uncurated clinical data to auto-delineate organs at risk, reducing delineation time by 31% (10.1 minutes, 95% confidence interval (CI) 7.4-12) with a median surface distance under 2.1 mm from clinician-corrected contours. This is a real prospective clinical workflow deployment with a measured time-efficiency benefit in a reproductive cancer setting, which is a stronger evidentiary bar than the retrospective segmentation studies typical of this literature.

Explain this simply

In a real hospital setting, an AI tool automatically outlined organs and tumors on scans for women receiving a type of internal radiation treatment for cervical cancer, cutting the time doctors spent manually drawing these outlines by about a third. The AI's outlines closely matched what doctors would draw themselves, needing only small corrections, showing a practical time-saving benefit for patients waiting with treatment devices already in place.

nnU-Net

A widely used deep learning model architecture designed to automatically outline structures in medical images.

Confidence Interval (CI)

A range of values that likely contains the true effect, giving a sense of how precise an estimate is.

Brachytherapy

A cancer treatment that places a radioactive source directly inside or next to the tumor.

Safety-Oriented Benchmarking of Large Language Models in Risk-Based Management of Abnormal Cervical Screening Results: Scenario-Based Benchmark Study.

J Med Internet Res · Eroğlu ÖO et al. · Sep 22, 2026

This benchmark study tested three large language models (LLMs) on 60 synthetic scenarios of abnormal cervical screening management against American Society for Colposcopy and Cervical Pathology (ASCCP) guidelines, finding unsafe major error-free rates ranging from 75 percent (DeepSeek V3.2) to 100 percent (GPT-5.3) under a guideline-directed prompt, with error rates rising sharply with scenario complexity. The blinded, gynecologist-rated safety framework with quantified error subtypes is a more rigorous evaluation than typical chatbot-quality rubrics, though scenarios are synthetic rather than real patient cases.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested whether AI chatbots could safely manage abnormal Pap smear results according to medical guidelines, using 60 made-up but realistic clinical situations, and found that model safety varied a lot, from perfect to worse in one model, and got worse as cases became more complicated. This is a more careful safety test than usual chatbot studies because doctors graded actual error types, but it used invented rather than real patient cases.

AI in OB/GYN and REI LLM Alignment Mismatch 7

The Alignment Paradox of Medical Large Language Models in Infertility Care: Decoupling Algorithmic Improvement From Clinical Decision-Making Quality.

J Med Internet Res · Liu D et al. · Sep 21, 2026

This retrospective study of 8201 infertility electronic health records compared four large language model (LLM) alignment strategies, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), group relative policy optimization (GRPO), and in-context learning (ICL), finding that GRPO scored highest on automatic metrics but blinded expert review by two reproductive medicine specialists rated the simpler SFT model higher on reasoning and treatment feasibility, with SFT selected as the best response in 51.2 percent of cases versus 26.2 percent for GRPO. This decoupling of automatic benchmark performance from expert clinical judgment is a genuinely useful and underappreciated finding for anyone building or evaluating clinical decision-support LLMs in reproductive medicine.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested four different ways of training AI models to help make infertility treatment decisions, and found that the method that scored best on automated tests was actually rated worse by real fertility doctors than a simpler training method. This shows that just optimizing an AI to hit high scores on standard tests doesn't necessarily make it better at real clinical reasoning, which matters a lot for anyone trying to build these tools for real use.

Large Language Model (LLM)

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

A method of training an AI model by showing it many examples of correct input-output pairs.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

A training method that adjusts an AI model based on which of two responses humans preferred.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

A reinforcement learning method that trains an AI model by comparing groups of its own responses against each other to improve reward-based performance.

In-Context Learning (ICL)

A way of guiding an AI model's behavior by giving it examples within the prompt itself, without changing its underlying training.

LLMs and Patient Communication LLM Diagnostic Bias 7

Implicit Bias in Large Language Model Diagnosis of Eating Disorders: Experimental Vignette Study.

JMIR AI · McCalla D et al. · Sep 23, 2026

This experimental vignette study tested 10 large language model (LLM) configurations on 1152 matched synthetic clinical cases and found substantial demographic bias in psychiatric diagnosis, including a 9.57 odds ratio for Latine patients receiving a major depressive disorder (MDD) diagnosis over identical White-patient vignettes, using mixed-effects logistic regression. This is a clear, well-controlled failure-mode demonstration relevant to the reader's interest in how language models err when reasoning about patients, though it is limited to synthetic vignettes rather than real clinical encounters.

Explain this simply

Researchers created nearly identical fake patient case descriptions that differed only in things like race or gender, then asked several AI chatbots to diagnose them, and found the AI gave very different diagnoses depending on demographic details, for example diagnosing depression far more often in Black and Latino patients than in white patients with the same symptoms. This shows a real bias problem in how AI handles patient information, which matters because it could affect real diagnoses if these tools are used in practice, though the patients here were not real.

Clinical AI Ambient Scribe Evaluation 6

Comparative Evaluation of Ambient Digital Scribe Systems in Clinical Documentation.

J Am Med Inform Assoc · Weiss E et al. · Sep 23, 2026

This study compared three commercial ambient digital scribe (ADS) systems on two simulated clinical encounters, pairing 56 physicians' Likert ratings with an independent transcript-level error audit, and found that the vendor with the highest clinician ratings was not the one with the fewest audited errors, suggesting clinicians' subjective judgments and objective accuracy diverge. The exploratory sample size and single simulated encounter type limit generalizability, but the mismatch between perceived quality and factual accuracy is a useful caution for anyone evaluating scribe tools by survey alone.

Explain this simply

Doctors compared three AI note-taking assistants that listen to patient visits and write clinical notes, rating them by feel and also carefully checking each note against the recording for actual mistakes. The tool doctors rated highest was not the one with the fewest real errors, showing that doctors' gut impressions of an AI tool do not always match how accurate it actually is.

Ambient Digital Scribe (ADS)

An AI system that listens to a patient visit and automatically writes a clinical note from the conversation.

Likert Scale

A rating scale, often 1 to 7, used to measure opinions or agreement rather than a hard fact.

Clinical AI HER2 Cancer Trial 6

Trastuzumab Plus Nivolumab with Gemcitabine and Cisplatin as First-Line Therapy for HER2-Positive Unresectable Biliary Tract Cancer: A Phase 1b/2 Trial.

Nat Med · Lee CK et al. · Sep 22, 2026

This phase 1b/2 trial of trastuzumab plus nivolumab with gemcitabine and cisplatin in HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2)-positive biliary tract cancer reported a 55% objective response rate and median progression-free survival of 10.6 months, with a preplanned artificial intelligence-powered whole-slide image analysis suggesting higher HER2 3+ cell proportions correlated with greater benefit. The trial itself is a substantive first-line readout worth tracking for oncology, but the AI component is a secondary, exploratory biomarker analysis rather than the study's main contribution.

Explain this simply

In a small trial, adding two immune and targeted drugs to standard chemotherapy for a bile duct cancer with a specific marker (HER2) caused tumors to shrink in over half of patients, a strong result for this hard-to-treat cancer. Researchers also used an AI image analysis tool on tumor slides as a side experiment, finding that tumors with more of the marker responded better, but this was a minor add-on to the main drug trial.

Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2)

A protein that some cancer cells overproduce, which can be targeted by specific drugs.

Objective Response Rate

Progression-free Survival

AI in OB/GYN and REI Lactation Drug Safety Modeling 6

A RISKmilk Framework for Predicting Drug and Chemical Exposure in Human Milk During Breastfeeding.

Pharmacol Res · Liu X et al. · Sep 24, 2026

Researchers built RISKmilk, a machine learning-enhanced physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model that predicts drug transfer into breast milk from molecular structure, improving the proportion of predictions within twofold error from 29.6% to 66.4% compared to a conventional equation-based benchmark, and applied it to screen marketed drugs for lactation risk. This is a methodologically substantive advance for an underserved population in drug safety modeling, though it remains a computational prediction tool without prospective clinical validation.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI-powered model that predicts how much of a drug passes into breast milk based on the drug's chemical structure, and it was much more accurate than the older calculation method doctors have relied on. They used it to flag which common medications might pass into milk at levels worth watching, which could help guide breastfeeding safety decisions, though it still needs to be tested against real-world clinical outcomes.

Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic (PBPK) Model

A computer model that simulates how a drug moves through the body using real biological structures and processes, rather than simple statistical curves.

Large Language Models Meet Gynecologic Ultrasound: Advancing the Characterization of ADNEXal Masses.

Investigators tested ChatGPT (GPT-5) on classifying 300 adnexal masses as benign or malignant against the IOTA (International Ovarian Tumor Analysis) Simple Rules, the ADNEX model, and expert subjective assessment, with the language model reaching about 73-75% accuracy compared to 87.3% for expert assessment. This is a genuine head-to-head comparison of a large language model (LLM) against established clinical decision tools and human experts, meeting the bar for the language-model beat, though the model underperformed experts and needs further validation before clinical use.

The AI got it right about three out of four times, noticeably worse than experienced doctors who were right about seven out of eight times, showing that current chatbots are not yet ready to replace expert judgment in this area.

AI in OB/GYN and REI Neonatal Injury Phenotypes 6

Distinct Clinical Patterns of Preterm Infants Vulnerable to Intestinal Injury Identified by Unsupervised Clustering.

Pediatr Res · Oh SH et al. · Sep 23, 2026

Using unsupervised hierarchical clustering on a prospective cohort of 20,352 very low birth weight infants from the Korean Neonatal Network, this study identified eight clinical clusters and five subclusters of intestinal injury independent of standard necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) diagnostic labels, with survival patterns validated against a supervised model. The large multicenter scale and diagnosis-independent phenotyping offer a potentially useful reframing of NEC heterogeneity, though the work remains exploratory and lacks external validation outside the network.

Explain this simply

Scientists analyzed data from over 20,000 premature babies to find natural groupings of intestinal injury patterns that don't match the usual diagnostic categories used today. The study is large and well-designed, but it's still an early step; the new groupings haven't yet been tested in a different hospital system.

Unsupervised Clustering

Prospective Cohort

Large Language Model-Based Triage to Identify Antiretroviral Therapy Adherence Barriers and Risk Levels in Patient Messages.

JAMIA Open · Ma Y et al. · Sep 26, 2026

Researchers fine-tuned large language models (LLMs), including Flan-T5 and clinical foundation models, on 15,480 annotated patient message sentences from people with HIV to detect antiretroviral therapy (ART) adherence barriers and stratify nonadherence risk, with Flan-T5-xl achieving a Macro-F1 of 0.83 internally and 0.71 on an external dataset while using roughly 30 times less energy than GPT-4.1. This is a real patient-message classification task with external validation and a fairness and environmental-footprint analysis, which is more substantive than a typical chatbot-quality rubric study, though it stops short of clinical deployment.

Explain this simply

Researchers trained AI models on real messages from people living with HIV to automatically flag reasons patients might skip their medication and how urgent the risk is, testing the models on messages from a different source to check they still worked. A smaller, more efficient model performed better and used far less energy than a bigger commercial AI model, and this kind of test on real patient text and outside data is more meaningful than typical chatbot grading studies.

LLMs and Patient Communication Medical Report Simplification 6

Improving Patient Communication and Understanding of Medical Reports Through Text Simplification with ChatGPT: A Comparative Study.

Oral Maxillofac Surg · Stephan D et al. · Sep 26, 2026

In this study, ChatGPT simplified five types of medical reports, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), surgical, pathology, and discharge summaries, and 576 laypeople without medical training were tested on comprehension of either the original or simplified version, with simplified reports improving comprehension accuracy substantially, for example from 53.7 percent to 80.3 percent on MRI reports. Unlike most chatbot-quality rubric studies, this measures an actual downstream effect on patient understanding with a large sample, which makes it more informative for this beat despite lacking a clinician-generated simplification comparator.

Explain this simply

Researchers used ChatGPT to rewrite complicated medical reports like MRI and surgery notes into simpler language, then tested nearly 600 people with no medical background to see if they understood the simplified versions better than the originals. People understood the simplified reports much better, for example correctly answering questions about MRI reports 80 percent of the time versus only 54 percent with the original wording, which is a real measured benefit rather than just a quality score.

Clinical AI Intraoperative Risk Prediction 5

A Real-Time Machine Learning Framework for Improved Intraoperative Risk Predictions in Cardiac Surgery Patients.

J Am Med Inform Assoc · Zhu D et al. · Sep 24, 2026

A retrospective single-center cohort of 6416 cardiac surgery patients trained an end-to-end transformer model (TE-Transformer) that ingests preoperative, intraoperative, and note data to produce continuously updated multitask risk predictions, reporting area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.85 for mortality and 0.88 for delirium, outperforming XGBoost and recurrent neural network baselines. The study lacks external or prospective validation, so its clinical utility for real-time decision support remains unproven despite the architectural novelty.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI system that continuously watches data during heart surgery, like vital signs and notes, to predict complications such as death, delirium, and kidney injury, testing it on over 6000 past surgeries at one hospital. It did better than older prediction methods, but since it was only tested on data from a single hospital without checking it works elsewhere, it is too early to know if it would help in real operating rooms.

Transformer

A type of deep learning architecture originally built for language tasks that can also process sequences of medical data over time.
