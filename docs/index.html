Clinical AI AI Genetic Diagnosis 9

AI-based clinician decision support system for diagnosis of inherited retinal diseases: a multicenter, randomized trial.

Nat Med · Jia H et al. · Jul 24, 2026

In a multicenter randomized controlled trial (n=295 with confirmed genotypes), a Vision Transformer-based clinician decision support system (Retina4IRD) pretrained with RETFound significantly improved top-5 genotype prediction accuracy for inherited retinal diseases (88.5% vs 67.3%, P<0.001) and improved downstream management scores when used alongside specialists versus specialists alone. This is a rare RCT with a hard clinical endpoint and external validation across three countries, representing a genuine first in AI-assisted genetic diagnosis workflow and a strong template for evaluating AI decision support broadly.

Explain this simply

Scientists built an AI tool that looks at eye scans and predicts which of 17 genetic eye diseases a patient likely has, then tested it in a real randomized trial with 295 patients. When doctors used the AI's suggestions, they correctly narrowed down the genetic diagnosis far more often (88.5% versus 67.3% of the time) and made better treatment decisions, which is a strong, well-tested example of AI actually improving patient care rather than just performing well on a computer test.

Clinical AI Speech Biomarker Validation 8

A deep learning model for speech-based prediction of clinical scores in people with Huntington's disease: a longitudinal study with cross-sectional replication.

Lancet Digit Health · Le Moine Veillon C et al. · Jul 23, 2026

NDSNet, a deep learning model combining wav2vec 2.0 embeddings with a recurrent attentive network in a contrastive learning framework, predicted Unified Huntington's Disease Rating Scale motor scores from speech across three prospective cohorts (191 patients, 58 controls) with an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.87, comparable to clinician ratings, and this held up in an independent external replication cohort of 110 patients. This is a rare example of a speech biomarker validated longitudinally and externally against a hard clinical rating scale and MRI-based atrophy, making it a credible progression-tracking tool rather than another single-cohort proof of concept.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI system that listens to speech recordings and estimates how far a person's Huntington's disease, a genetic brain disorder, has progressed, matching the accuracy of a doctor's own clinical rating in three separate patient groups followed over time and a fourth independent group used to double check the results. This matters because it shows speech alone, something easy and cheap to collect, can reliably track disease severity, offering a much less invasive way to monitor progression than brain scans or in-person exams.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Cancer Imaging 6

Multi-modal Stacking Machine Learning Model for Predicting Lymph Node Metastasis in Cervical Cancer.

Acad Radiol · Yang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

A multimodal stacking ensemble combining intratumoral and peritumoral MRI radiomic habitats with clinicopathological variables predicted lymph node metastasis in early-stage cervical cancer, reaching AUCs of 0.915, 0.895, and 0.875 in training, internal, and external validation cohorts, outperforming all base learners by DeLong test. The design includes external validation and decision curve analysis, which strengthens clinical relevance, though it remains retrospective and radiomics pipelines historically generalize poorly across scanners and institutions.

Explain this simply

Researchers built a machine learning model that combines MRI image patterns from inside and around cervical tumors with patient clinical data to predict whether cancer has spread to lymph nodes, and it correctly distinguished cases about 85 to 92 percent of the time depending on the patient group tested. The study tested the model on a separate group of patients from a different hospital, which is a meaningful check, but this kind of image analysis often performs worse when used on real-world scanners and populations it hasn't seen before.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Screening Efficiency 6

Improving laboratory workforce efficiency using AI-assisted digital cytology within an HPV-based cervical screening programme: A model-based evaluation for the NHS Cervical Screening Programmes.

BMJ Open · Wilson A et al. · Jul 28, 2026

A decision-tree modeling study estimated that AI-assisted digital cytology could cut mean slide review time from 12.9 to 4.0 minutes in the NHS HPV-triage cervical screening workflow, translating to a projected 69% gain in laboratory productivity across roughly 480,000 annual slides. This is a health-economic simulation using published performance data rather than a prospective trial, so the workforce savings are only as credible as the underlying assumptions, but it is directly relevant to scaling AI-assisted screening in practice.

Explain this simply

Researchers used a computer model to estimate how much staff time a national cervical screening program in England could save by using AI-assisted digital microscopy instead of manual review, and found it could cut review time per sample from about 13 minutes to 4 minutes, potentially boosting overall lab output by 69 percent. This is not a real-world test but a projection based on existing data, so the real savings could differ, though it's a useful estimate for health system planning given ongoing lab staffing shortages.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Screening AI 6

An explainable machine learning approach for cervical cancer screening: decoding morphological diagnostic drivers.

Acta Clin Belg · Chang H et al. · Jul 29, 2026

An explainable ML pipeline for liquid-based cytology screening achieved 92.5% sensitivity for detecting high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions or worse versus 84.2% for manual cytopathologist review in a retrospective double-blind comparison of 5,000 women, with AUC 0.94 and no significant performance drop across menopausal or infection subgroups. The SHAP-based feature analysis adds interpretability and the efficiency gains are substantial, but the study is retrospective and single-network, so prospective multi-site validation is still needed before this changes screening practice broadly.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested an AI system that reads cervical cancer screening slides against a panel of expert pathologists on 5,000 women's samples, and the AI caught more true cases of serious pre-cancerous changes, 92.5 percent versus 84.2 percent for the human reviewers, while also working equally well regardless of a patient's menopause status or infections. The AI also explained which cell features it used to make its decisions and cut review time significantly, but since this was a retrospective study done at one healthcare network in China, it still needs to be tested prospectively and in other settings before it can be trusted for widespread use.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Cervical Cancer Prognosis 6

Non-contrast MRI Radiomics Nomogram for Predicting Recurrence and Progression-Free Survival in Cervical Cancer following Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy.

Curr Med Imaging · Wang W et al. · Jul 23, 2026

A two-center retrospective study built a non-contrast MRI radiomics nomogram using an XGBoost classifier to predict recurrence and progression-free survival after chemoradiotherapy for cervical cancer, with significant risk stratification in both the 114-patient construction cohort (P<0.001) and 31-patient external validation cohort (P=0.012), performing comparably to contrast-enhanced models. The external validation and avoidance of gadolinium contrast are genuine strengths, though the validation cohort is small and single-institution pairs limit generalizability.

Explain this simply

Researchers built an AI tool that reads standard MRI scans, without needing an injected contrast dye, to predict which cervical cancer patients are likely to have their cancer come back after treatment. The tool worked about as well as more complex contrast-based scans and was tested on a separate small group of patients, which is a meaningful step, though the test group was still quite small.

Clinical AI AI Psychosis Screening 5

Evaluating large language models for assessment of psychosis risk.

NPJ Digit Med · Zhu T et al. · Jul 23, 2026

Eleven open-weight large language models were evaluated on 678 partial psychiatric interview transcripts from 373 participants to infer clinical high-risk psychosis status, with the best model (Llama-3.3-70B) reaching 0.80 accuracy, 0.93 sensitivity, and 0.58 specificity, and symptom severity scores correlating well with human raters (intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.74-0.75). This is a promising but exploratory benchmarking study without prospective clinical deployment or outcome data, so its value lies mainly in demonstrating feasibility rather than establishing clinical utility.

Explain this simply

Researchers tested eleven AI language models to see if they could read transcripts of psychiatric interviews and correctly judge whether someone is at high risk for psychosis, and the best one matched trained clinicians fairly well, catching 93% of true risk cases but flagging many false positives too. This shows AI might help scale up early detection of psychosis risk, but the study only tested this in a research setting rather than in actual clinical use.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Birth Weight Prediction 5

Predictive modeling with machine learning for low birth weight in the state of São Paulo: a retrospective population-based study.

Sao Paulo Med J · Almeida AVA et al. · Jul 24, 2026

Using 2.5 million live births from Sao Paulo state, this retrospective study trained logistic regression and random forest models to predict low birth weight, with the best model achieving AUC-ROC 0.850 and sensitivity 0.688 on an 80/20 train-test split. The population scale is a strength but the lack of external validation and the use of only a held-out split from the same source limit confidence that this generalizes beyond the study population.

Explain this simply

Using data from over 2.5 million births in a Brazilian state, researchers built computer models to predict which babies would be born underweight, and the best model correctly flagged about 69 percent of true low-weight cases while achieving a reasonably strong overall accuracy score. The dataset is impressively large, but the model was only tested on a portion of the same dataset it was trained on, not on a fully separate population, so it's unclear how well it would work elsewhere.

AI in Ob/Gyn and REI Oocyte Quality AI 5

AI-derived oocyte morphology and follicular fluid biomarkers in donors.

Reproduction · Herrero Y et al. · Jul 29, 2026

In a small cohort of 49 young oocyte donors, AI-derived oocyte morphology scores correlated with specific follicular fluid biomarkers including glucose, HDL, AMH, and HSPG2, providing a biological basis for what AI image scores may be capturing about oocyte competence. The sample size is modest and the design correlational, so this is exploratory hypothesis-generating work rather than a validated clinical tool, but it directly addresses the interpretability of AI oocyte grading, which matters for REI researchers.

Explain this simply

Researchers compared AI-generated quality scores for egg cells with chemical markers measured in the fluid surrounding those eggs in 49 young egg donors, and found that higher AI scores were linked to certain markers like cholesterol, a hormone called AMH, and a structural protein, suggesting these AI scores reflect real biological features rather than just visual patterns. The study is small and only shows correlations, not proof that the AI score improves outcomes, but it helps explain what these increasingly used AI grading tools might actually be measuring biologically.
